# CLAUDE.md: Traffic

A traffic game built in Unity 6 (project version 6000.6.3f1) on Windows 11. The scripts run under the Built-in Render Pipeline or URP: `MeshKit` picks each pipeline's own shaders, and the debug lines are a mesh, not `GL` calls, because URP does not call `OnRenderObject`. The current build is the Driver Lab: a few maps for testing individual driver behavior, and a road builder where the player draws their own. Puzzle levels, zones and events come later. The earlier web version (TypeScript and three.js) was removed once the Unity lab worked; it is in the git history.

## Layout

- `Assets/Scripts/Sim/`: the simulation (namespace `Traffic.Sim`). It has no Unity references, runs on a fixed 1/30 s step (`Simulation.Dt`) with a seeded `Rng`, and is replayable from a seed.
  - `Simulation.cs`: each step does signal updates, per-lane car lists, `Perceive` (each car picks an acceleration), lane changes, integration, crash detection.
  - `Driver.cs`: the six driver dials (`DriverParams`) plus three quirks (`Weave`, `Swerve`, `Distraction`), values derived from them, the IDM car-following formula, and the `SpecialDriver` presets (maniac, slowpoke, on phone, drunk).
  - `Vehicle.cs`: `VehicleSpec` for cars, trucks and motorcycles: size, and multipliers on acceleration, braking, speed, cornering and lane-change time. `Simulation.DeriveFor(car)` combines driver and vehicle. Vehicles longer than 6 m are posed along the line from tail to nose so they do not swing into the next lane in turns. (No vehicle is that long at the moment: the truck is a 5 m delivery truck, sized from its model.)
  - `Network.cs`: `Lane`, `Node`, `Approach` and `World`. Paths through an intersection are lanes of kind `Connector`; `Connectors.Link` finds where they conflict.
  - `NetworkBuilder.cs`: builds a network from nodes joined by roads. A road is straight unless given via points (`AddCurvedRoad` takes a traced centerline and trims it at each end), may be one-way, and has a speed limit. An intersection grows to fit roads that meet at a narrow angle, so they have separated by their stop lines; `Build` throws if that leaves under 15 m of road between two intersections. Each direction is a set of lanes in slots (0 next to the centerline); a lane can start late (turn pocket, added lane) or end early (merge). It generates connectors, turn assignments, pavement and paint. A node with one road is the edge of the map (`Node.Terminal`): arriving lanes are exits (`Lane.IsExit`) and leaving lanes are entries (`World.Entries`). A `Free` node with three or more roads is a ramp leaving or joining (ramps use `shiftA`/`shiftB` to attach at the highway's outside lane) or, with no ramp, a give-way junction such as a roundabout's, kept as small as its roads allow. `inner` moves every lane of a road sideways from its centerline: half a median's width divides the two directions (the strip between goes in `World.Medians`), and on a one-way road minus half its width centers the lanes on the line.
  - `Maps.cs`: the maps the lab can load (`Maps.All`): the street grid, the figure eight, and a town joined to a divided highway by a diamond interchange (highway through lanes are given `Turn.Straight` explicitly so they are not offered the ramps on the far side). `Simulation.Arrivals` brings vehicles in at entries at a rate that rises and falls; vehicles that run off an exit lane are removed and counted in `Left`.
  - `EditableMap.cs`: the map the player draws: points joined by roads at any angle, each straight or curved (part of a circle, stored as its two ends and the point halfway along, `EditSegment.Mid`; a curve may turn up to 240 degrees), and of a `RoadType`. `RoadType.All` is what the toolbar offers: Street (25 mph), Avenue (40 mph, the two-way ones with a median) and Highway (65 mph, one-way, one to four lanes), each with lanes each way, a tightest curve and a price per meter; `EditableMap.Cost` totals the drawing. `RoadType.Plain(lanes, mph)` is the older way to describe a road, which most tests and older saved drawings use. `TryAdd` snaps ends, makes an intersection where a new road crosses an old one, splits a road that is drawn to or from (a curve splits into two pieces of the same circle), and refuses roads that are too tight for their type, doubled, or that meet another at under 40 degrees. There is no minimum length for a piece (the player asked for it to go): what is checked is how things tie together. Pieces of the same kind that meet with nothing else there are one road to the network (`Runs`: a `Run` goes from one junction or road end to the next, and `Lay` rounds each corner between its pieces, taking up to one and a half times the type's tightest radius where there is room). A change that leaves a corner tighter than the type allows is refused (`SharpCorners`, `Unbuildable`), as is one that leaves junctions too close for the builder. A ring of pieces with no junction on it becomes a road from one point back to itself. `Faults` lists places where traffic could not flow (it arrives with no road leading away, or a road has nothing leading in, because of the way one-way roads run); these are shown, not refused, since a junction is often only right once its last road is drawn. A one-way road that meets another road at under 40 degrees, on the right of the traffic it joins or leaves, makes a merge or an exit, not an intersection (`JoinAt`): the node is `Free`, the ramp is reshaped to come in beside the other road's lanes (`BesideTheRoad`, built with `NetworkBuilder.AddRamp`), and past a merge the road gains a lane for each ramp lane, which ends further on. `TryReplace` changes a road to another type in place. `Bend` gives the curve that leaves a point heading a given way and ends at another (`BendArrives` is the heading it arrives with, `FlowAt` the heading of a road at its open end), which is how curves are drawn so that each piece carries on from the last without a corner. `TryMove` moves a point and the ends of its roads with it (a curve keeps its shape) and `TryBend` bends a road through a point or straightens it; neither joins or crosses roads, which only drawing does. A piece can carry extra lanes (`EditSegment.Extras`, each an `ExtraLane`: a side and where it starts and ends along the piece): `TryAddLane` lays one between two points on the same road, outside the ordinary lanes on the side of the first point, and `TryRemoveLane` takes one away. Extra lanes on neighboring pieces that meet end to end are one lane (`ExtrasAlong`), and one that all but reaches the end of its road runs right to it and becomes a lane of the junction there. They are built as the builder's lanes that start late and end early, so traffic moves into them and back out as it does at a merge. `TryAddRoundabout` lays a one-way, one-lane ring (counter-clockwise, its pieces flagged `EditSegment.Ring`), cuts the roads that cross it and removes what is inside; nodes on a ring are `Free`, so turning traffic gives way to traffic going round. `ToWorld` builds everything else with automatic control: a bend where two different kinds of road meet, all-way stops where only 25 mph streets meet, a light otherwise; an open road end is a map edge. Roads are added to the builder in the order their first pieces were drawn; the traffic tests are sensitive to that order. It saves as one line of text.
  - `Routes.cs`: for each way off the map, the seconds from every lane to it, and so the turn to take at each junction and whether one edge of the map can reach another. With `Simulation.UseRoutes` on, each arriving vehicle picks a destination it can reach (from `Simulation.Demand`, or any other exit) and follows the quickest route; `Arrived`, `TripSeconds` and `TripIdeal` record finished journeys. Off, vehicles turn at random, which is what the lab maps and most tests use.
  - `Levels.cs`: the puzzles (`Levels.All`). A `Level` lays its own roads (locked: `EditSegment.Locked`, free, not removable) coming in at named places (`EditableMap.Ends`, which become `Node.Name`) round a building area (`EditableMap.Bound`), lists the journeys traffic wants, and has a budget. `Connected` counts the journeys the roads make possible; `Stars` is 0 until every journey is possible, the cost is within budget and enough have been finished, then 1 to 3 by how little longer journeys took than on empty roads.
  - `LabWorld.cs`: the lab grid, described in its header comment. `Junction.cs`: one controller per node, with modes `Free` (a bend), `Signal`, `ProtectedLeft` and `AllWay`.
- `Assets/Scripts/Lab/`: the Unity side. `LabBootstrap` creates a `LabApp` when Play is pressed, in any scene, so there is no scene file. Meshes, materials, camera and light are built in code (`MeshKit`, `RoadView`, `CarViews`). `RoadView` lays the ground and the roads in textured surfaces (`SurfaceBuilder`): asphalt mapped from its place on the map, a gravel shoulder on roads of 55 mph and over, a curbed grass median, and crosswalk bars at intersections with a light or signs. Sidewalks (a raised walk with a curb along slower roads and round their corners, `Node.Corners`) are switched off (`RoadView.Sidewalks`): the player wants the zones of a city to lay them later. The roads are meant to look newly built: even dark asphalt, bright paint and no wear (the player asked for the wheel tracks and grime to go). Under each tree goes soft dark shading (`ShadeBuilder`, drawn with `MeshKit.Shade`, which only ever darkens). Scenery (trees and bushes as lumpy leaf-covered tops from `CanopyBuilder`, all in summer greens close enough to read as one color, and rocks) is built in square patches whose meshes are kept between rebuilds; each square of ground decides what grows on it from its own position, so scenery stays put when roads change. "Draw your own" in the map list opens the player's `EditableMap` in build mode and rebuilds the world after every edit. All the drawing itself is done by `RoadPen` (in `Ui/`, below): `LabApp` passes it the pointer's place on the ground, clicks and drags, rebuilds the world when it says the map changed, and copies its `Ghost` into a mesh each time that changes (`ShowGhost`). The left button only builds and picks things, and acts as it goes down (with the move tool, a press on a point or a road drags that). The right button drags the view, and a right-click that does not drag takes back the last click; the wheel zooms about the pointer; the middle button, or Q and E, turns the view (`yaw`, `tilt`), W, A, S, D and the arrows move it, and N puts north back up. Keys that act while held are tracked from GUI key events (`held`). While building, a handful of cars drive around (`AmbientCars`) and are put back on their roads after each rebuild (`Carry`); Drive starts full traffic. The drawing is saved in `PlayerPrefs`, with undo and redo kept as saved copies. A puzzle level opens the same way (`LoadLevel`), with its place names, the edge of its building area, cost against budget and routes joined shown, and a card with its stars once solved. The UI is immediate-mode GUI (`LabUi.cs`) and reads mouse and keyboard from GUI events, so it works under either input setting. Anything in the UI that changes which controls a panel holds (loading a map, Build, Drive) is put in `later` and done in `Update`, because changing it between GUI passes makes Unity throw.
- `Assets/Scripts/Ui/`: the toolbar at the bottom of the screen and the hand that draws roads (namespace `Traffic.Ui`), with no Unity references. `RoadPen` holds the map being built, the tool, and the road half drawn, and turns clicks and drags into changes to the map. A straight road is a chain of clicks. A curve leaves the last point the way the road was already heading and sweeps round to the pointer; from a point with no single road to carry on from, one click first points it. Right-click goes back a step. The end of a road catches on roads and points within reach (in meters, or a few pixels if that is more), and a straight road nearly in line with the roads it starts from (straight on, square, halfway) is pulled into line when angle snapping is on. The move tool drags points and bends roads. The lane tool adds a lane along the side of a road with two clicks, and removes an added lane with one. Places `EditableMap.Faults` reports are ringed in red and explained under the pointer. The pen keeps a `Ghost`: flat colored triangles showing what the next click would do (the road at its real width, blue if it can be built and red if not, arrows on a one-way road, pins where it starts and ends, handles for the move tool), sized so outlines stay a few pixels wide however far out the view is, and a `Note` for beside the pointer (length, radius and price, or the reason it cannot be built). `Dock` lays out and draws the tools (road, lane, move, replace, bulldoze), the tray of choices (shape, kind of road from `Dock.Roads`, which `LabApp.RoadTiles` fills from `RoadType.All`, height, snapping) and the hover cards onto an `ISurface`; `LabUi.GuiSurface` is the Unity one, using the `GUI.DrawTexture` overload that rounds corners. `Icons` draws each icon in code with `Painter`.
- Textures: the ground, asphalt, sidewalk, gravel and foliage are made by `Tools/BakeArt/textures.py` from CC0 photo scans in `Art/ambientcg/`, each scan repeated small for close-up detail, moved to the color wanted on screen (`toned`), with only a faint slow drift of tone over it so the repeat does not show: fresh dark asphalt, clean concrete, one even green for the grass. `LabApp.SetUpScene` sets the sun and sky light so that flat ground in the open shows a texture at about its own brightness (measured from a player's screenshot, where the project turned out to be in gamma color space: lit color is texture color times sun plus ambient). Keep that sum near 1 if the light is changed, or every color shifts. They are saved as JPEG data named `.bytes` in `Assets/Resources/Art/` and decoded by `ArtKit.Photo`, so Unity's texture import settings do not matter. Missing ones fall back to plain colors.
- Art: cars, trucks and rocks are CC0 models from Kenney's Car Kit and Nature Kit (the kit's trees are baked too but no longer drawn). The sources used are in `Art/` (outside `Assets/`, so Unity ignores them). `Tools/BakeArt/bake.py` (Python with numpy and Pillow) turns them into `Assets/Resources/Art/models.bytes`: vehicles scaled by one factor for the whole kit (`KIT_SCALE`, so they keep their proportions; they come out slightly shorter and wider than the simulation's 4.5 by 1.9 m car, and the truck's simulated size, 5.0 by 2.3 m, was set from the model) and facing +x, each face marked as paint (takes the vehicle's color), leaf (takes a leaf color) or a fixed palette color. `Assets/Scripts/Art/ArtFile.cs` reads the file (no Unity references) and `Lab/ArtKit.cs` builds meshes from it, all sharing one small point-filtered texture of flat colors. Nothing goes through Unity's model importer. If the file is missing, vehicles and trees fall back to plain shapes. Motorcycles are still plain shapes.
- `Assets/Tests/Sim/`: NUnit tests of the simulation.

## Behavior notes

- Right of way inside an intersection is a rank (`Simulation.Rank`). At a `Free` node (a way on to a roundabout) traffic going straight on outranks traffic turning, and a turning car waits short of the traffic going by for as long as it takes: the `BoxWait` rule below does not apply there. A red-light runner waits for a gap, except a pushy one (`IsBully`), who outranks everyone once past the line; ordinary drivers who see that brake hard (`Scared`).
- A car only counts as inside an intersection once its nose is `Committed` (0.5 m) past the stop line. Cars outside hold at the line while someone is sitting in their path, and a car stuck inside stops giving way after a while (`BoxWait`). These three rules exist to prevent standstills; the stress probes that found them ran 60 to 120 cars with special drivers for 10 simulated minutes.
- Roads follow US practice: 12 ft lanes (`LaneW` 3.66 m), 10 ft dashes with 30 ft gaps, and limits of 25, 35 and 65 mph (`CitySpeed`, `ArterialSpeed`, `HighwaySpeed`).
- A drawn road has a `Level`: 0 on the ground, 1 to 3 for a bridge, -1 to -3 for a tunnel, 5 m a level, reached over a ramp at each end (`EditableMap.RampFor`) and costing more (`CostTimes`). It crosses other roads without an intersection where there is 4.5 m between them, and can only be joined at its ends. `NetworkBuilder.Raise` lifts or sinks the road just added; strips and paint carry a height per point (`Heights`). `RoadView` draws a bridge as a deck with walls, and a tunnel as open road until it is 2.4 m down (`Covered`), then broken lines on the ground with a dark mouth at each end; `CarViews` hides cars that deep.
- A road added with `bridge: true` rises over whatever it crosses (`Lane.Peak`, `Car.Height`). Roads only interact at nodes, so traffic on and under a bridge ignores each other; crash detection skips pairs at different heights.
- `Node.Controlled` means a real intersection with a light or stop signs. Bends, ramp junctions and map edges are not controlled: no stop lines, no signals, not selectable.
- A driver changing lanes looks for the car ahead and the car behind in the next lane through the junction at either end and into the road beyond (`LeaderIn`, `FollowerIn`, `LookBack`), and ignores a car that is already moving out of that lane. Without this, drivers near a merge pulled out in front of fast traffic they could not see, or into a queue just past the junction.
- Accidents (`Simulation.Collide`): vehicles that overlap crash. Closing speed decides whether it is a fender bender (both stop, then drive on) or a wreck (blocks the road until towed, after `TowAfter`); the angle between them gives the `CrashKind`. Drivers go round a wreck when there is another lane (`Car.BlockedAhead`), and slow to look at one nearby (`Gawking`). With `CrashesEnabled` off, a car that would rear-end another is held behind it and counted in `Bumps`, and cars crossing paths pass through each other.

## Conventions

- A car's `S` is the position of its center along its lane. Traffic drives on the right.
- Sim units are meters and seconds; the UI shows mph and feet. Sim (x east, y north) maps to Unity (x, height, z = y); models face +x.
- Keep behavior deterministic: all randomness goes through `Simulation.Rng`.
- `SignalLight` and `PathPose` are named to avoid clashing with `UnityEngine.Light` and `UnityEngine.Pose`.
- Keep the C# to language version 9. Avoid Unity APIs marked obsolete in 6000.6 (for example `FindObjectsByType` with a sort mode).

## Checks

There is no Unity editor in the cloud container, so nothing here proves how the game looks or that it compiles in the installed Unity. With the .NET 8 SDK (`apt-get install -y dotnet-sdk-8.0`):

- `dotnet test Tools/SimTests` runs the simulation tests, and two files beside the project file that test code outside the simulation's assembly: `RoadPenTests.cs`, which draws roads click by click through the pen, and `ArtFileTests.cs`, which checks the baked art file (vehicles fit the simulation's sizes, which way they face, triangles facing outward).
- `python3 Tools/BakeArt/bake.py preview.png` rebakes the art and draws every model to a picture. `python3 Tools/BakeArt/textures.py preview.png` rebakes the textures and draws a sample of each with a mock-up of a road made of them (a guide to the intended look, not a render of the game).
- `dotnet build Tools/LabCompile` type-checks all scripts against Unity 2021.3 reference assemblies. It catches typos and type errors only.
- `dotnet run --project Tools/UiPreview out.html` draws the toolbar in each state, every icon, and the road pen at work (its ghost over a small map, at each step of drawing, moving and replacing) to a web page (text in Arial, close to Unity's default GUI font). Screenshot it to check a change to the toolbar or to how drawing looks.

Unity generates `Packages/`, the rest of `ProjectSettings/` and the `.meta` files on first open. They are not committed yet.
