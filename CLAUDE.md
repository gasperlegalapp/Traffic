# CLAUDE.md: Traffic

A traffic game built in Unity 6 (project version 6000.6.3f1) on Windows 11. The scripts run under the Built-in Render Pipeline or URP: `MeshKit` picks each pipeline's own shaders, and the debug lines are a mesh, not `GL` calls, because URP does not call `OnRenderObject`. The current build is the Driver Lab: a few maps for testing individual driver behavior, and a road builder where the player draws their own. Puzzle levels, zones and events come later. The earlier web version (TypeScript and three.js) was removed once the Unity lab worked; it is in the git history.

## Layout

- `Assets/Scripts/Sim/`: the simulation (namespace `Traffic.Sim`). It has no Unity references, runs on a fixed 1/30 s step (`Simulation.Dt`) with a seeded `Rng`, and is replayable from a seed.
  - `Simulation.cs`: each step does signal updates, per-lane car lists, `Perceive` (each car picks an acceleration), lane changes, integration, crash detection.
  - `Driver.cs`: the six driver dials (`DriverParams`) plus four quirks (`Weave`, `Swerve`, `Distraction`, `Camp`), values derived from them, the IDM car-following formula, and the `SpecialDriver` presets (maniac, slowpoke, on phone, drunk, left-lane camper). The camper makes for the leftmost lane that goes their way and sits there at 0.82 of the limit; the player asked for them so that other drivers have to go round. `Simulation.ArrivalCampers` is the share of arriving drivers who are campers (none unless asked for; the lab asks for 4%, a puzzle for none).
  - `Vehicle.cs`: `VehicleSpec` for cars, delivery trucks, motorcycles, box trucks and semis: size, and multipliers on acceleration, braking, speed, cornering and lane-change time. `Simulation.DeriveFor(car)` combines driver and vehicle. Vehicles longer than 6 m (the 7.6 m box truck, the 14 m semi) are posed along the line from tail to nose so they do not swing into the next lane in turns. A semi has a `Trailer` (the last 9.6 m of it): the place where tractor and trailer join keeps to the path as well, so each lies along its own stretch of road (`Car.CabX` and `TowX` and the rest, set in `UpdatePose`), and crashes are judged on the two pieces separately (`Boxes`), with the trailer counted a little slim because it cuts the inside of a corner. `Simulation.BigTrucks` is the share of arriving trucks that are box trucks or semis; it is taken from the same throw of the dice as the kind of vehicle, and is 0 unless asked for, so traffic in the older tests is as it was.
  - `Network.cs`: `Lane`, `Node`, `Approach` and `World`. Paths through an intersection are lanes of kind `Connector`; `Connectors.Link` finds where they conflict.
  - `NetworkBuilder.cs`: builds a network from nodes joined by roads. A road is straight unless given via points (`AddCurvedRoad` takes a traced centerline and trims it at each end), may be one-way, and has a speed limit. An intersection grows to fit roads that meet at a narrow angle, so they have separated by their stop lines; `Build` throws if that leaves under 15 m of road between two intersections with a light or signs, or if two intersections overlap. A bend, or a point where roads were built on from lanes, is soft: it gives up its own length to a short road beside it (down to `SoftRadius`, with as little as `ShortRoad`, 3.5 m, of road between two such places), because the player wants to lay pieces as small as they like and was being told "the junctions would be too close together". That check on overlap still holds (when both are large the road between them comes out long again, back to front, so the length check alone misses it). Each direction is a set of lanes in slots (0 next to the centerline); a lane can start late (turn pocket, added lane) or end early (merge). It generates connectors, turn assignments, pavement and paint. A node with one road is the edge of the map (`Node.Terminal`): arriving lanes are exits (`Lane.IsExit`) and leaving lanes are entries (`World.Entries`). Marked with `DeadEnd` it is instead a road that just stops (`Node.DeadEnd`, `BuildRoadEnd`): each arriving lane pulls forward, swings round to the left across the middle of the road and comes back into the lane it mirrors (a path of kind straight on, so trucks may take it). Its lanes stop a few meters short of the end of the pavement to leave room for that, where the road is long enough, but the paint runs on to the very end (edge lines, yellow line, lane lines), so the turn is made across the yellow line on what looks like plain road. A one-way road's lanes run right to its end, where its traffic leaves the map. The player first got a round turning place there and disliked it strongly, then a red and white board across the end with bare pavement before it and asked for both to go: a road that stops is just a road nobody has carried on yet ("the road is a road"), and must not get in the way of building on from it. A `Free` node with three or more roads is a ramp leaving or joining (ramps use `shiftA`/`shiftB` to attach at the highway's outside lane) or, with no ramp, a give-way junction such as a roundabout's, kept as small as its roads allow. `inner` moves every lane of a road sideways from its centerline: half a median's width divides the two directions (the strip between goes in `World.Medians`), and on a one-way road minus half its width centers the lanes on the line. A node with two roads is a bend, where one kind of road carries on as another: `BuildBend` gives it pavement whose two sides follow the same curves as the lines painted round it (`BendCurve`), and sets `Node.Hub`, a point inside the pavement to draw it from, since the node itself can lie outside a narrow road's bend. (The pavement used to be a pointed lump sticking out past the edge line.) `BuildBox` rounds the corner between two neighboring roads toward the point where their edges would meet, but only if that point is close: edges that run nearly side by side meet far away, and the corner once came out as a spike of pavement and curb tens of meters long, which the player saw as a white line across an avenue. Where two paths through a junction with no light end in the same lane, `PaintJunction` paints no line between them. `AssignStreets` decides which arms of an intersection share a green: two arms nearly opposite each other (within 25 degrees) are one road and so one street, and whatever crosses or joins it is the other; only where no road runs straight through does it fall back on sorting arms by direction. Sorting by direction alone put two roads that cross at under 45 degrees in one street, so both had green together.
  - `Maps.cs`: the maps the lab can load (`Maps.All`): the street grid, the figure eight, and a town joined to a divided highway by a diamond interchange (highway through lanes are given `Turn.Straight` explicitly so they are not offered the ramps on the far side). `Simulation.Arrivals` brings vehicles in at entries at a rate that rises and falls, slowly enough to stop where the entry road is under 45 m long; vehicles that run off an exit lane are removed and counted in `Left`.
  - `EditableMap.cs`: the map the player draws: points joined by roads at any angle, each straight or curved (part of a circle, stored as its two ends and the point halfway along, `EditSegment.Mid`; a curve may turn up to 240 degrees), and of a `RoadType`. `RoadType.Toolbar` is what the toolbar offers: a Street (25 mph) and an Avenue (40 mph) each as one lane one-way, two lanes one-way, one lane each way and two lanes each way; and a Highway (65 mph) one-way with one to four lanes, or two lanes each way with a median. What lies between the two directions of a two-way street or avenue is not a tile of its own: the player picks it for the whole class from a list that opens above the class's heading (`Dock.Middle`, `DockKind.Middle` and `MiddleChoice`; `RoadType.ToolbarMiddles`: a painted line, a turning lane, grass, or trees), and `RoadType.Offered` makes the road. They saw that list in another game and asked for it. A median with trees is `RoadType.Planted` (code ending `-r`, `Strip.Planted`, small trees along it in `RoadView`). `RoadType.All` is every road with a short code of its own, which is the toolbar's and a few more (`a2`, `a4b`) that are reached by changing a road or kept so older drawings load. Each has lanes each way, a tightest curve and a price per meter; `EditableMap.Cost` totals the drawing. `RoadType.Plain(lanes, mph)` is the older way to describe a road, which most tests and older saved drawings use. `TryAdd` snaps ends, makes an intersection where a new road crosses an old one, splits a road that is drawn to or from (a curve splits into two pieces of the same circle), and refuses roads that are too tight for their type, that add nothing new, that lie alongside another road on top of it (`Alongside`), or that meet another at under 30 degrees (`MinAngle`; it was 40, which made a road across the corner of a block all but impossible, since it meets one side at 90 degrees less the angle it meets the other). There is no minimum length for a piece beyond `MinPiece`, 6 m (the player asked for it to go, and later for little roads, "granular, very precise"): the far end of a road is not pulled back to the point it starts from once it is that far away (`SnapAway`, and the same in `RoadPen.Snapped`), where it used to fall back on it from anywhere within the 13 m reach of a click. What is checked is how things tie together. Pieces of the same kind that meet with nothing else there are one road to the network (`Runs`: a `Run` goes from one junction or road end to the next, and `Lay` rounds each corner between its pieces. Two straight pieces of a street or avenue meet in a tight corner, like the corner of a city block (`SquareCorners`, `CornerRadius`: the inside edge of the road turns on 5 m), because the player lays out grids with the straight tool and asked for sharp corners, not sweeps; a slight change of direction still gets a longer curve. Where a curved piece is involved, or on a highway, the corner takes up to one and a half times the type's tightest radius where there is room). A corner that there is no room to round off (`Run.Sharp`; a tight corner only needs the inside edge of the road to have a meter to turn on) is not refused any more: `Runs` cuts the road there, and the two pieces meet in a bend as two different kinds of road would. It was refused as "too sharp a corner", which the player met again and again once pieces could be short. A change that leaves junctions too close for the builder is still refused. A ring of pieces with no junction on it becomes a road from one point back to itself: the point where the ring turns least, which stays a bend while the other corners are rounded. `Faults` lists places where traffic could not flow (it arrives with no road leading away, or a road has nothing leading in, because of the way one-way roads run); these are shown, not refused, since a junction is often only right once its last road is drawn. A one-way road that meets another road at under 40 degrees (`MergeAngle`), on the right of the traffic it joins or leaves, makes a merge or an exit, not an intersection (`JoinAt`): the node is `Free`, the ramp is reshaped to come in beside the other road's lanes (`BesideTheRoad`, built with `NetworkBuilder.AddRamp`). The road does not gain a lane past a merge: it used to, for 150 m, and the player took it for the game building two lanes when one was picked. The rule they gave is that the game builds only what is selected on the toolbar; a lane for the ramp to run on in is theirs to add with the lane tool. The ramp's traffic gives way and moves into the outside lane. One-way roads that all run through a point nearly the same way, some arriving and some leaving, make a fork or come together there (`FlowsThrough`): no light or sign, and they may lie as close as 14 degrees. Where one road arrives at a fork its lanes are dealt out from left to right among the roads that leave (`ForkLanes`); where roads come together the builder keeps each to its own side. Lanes are told the roads they lead on to (`TrackSpec.Onto`), not turns, because at a fork or a bending exit the builder may not call the same road "straight on" as the map would. A lane that leads to an intersection can be told which ways it may go there (`TrySetTurn`, kept per lane in `EditSegment.TurnsAtA` and `TurnsAtB` and passed to the builder as explicit turns): that is how a lane becomes a left-turn lane, or a turn is forbidden; `LaneTurns` reads what a lane may do now from the built network. Joining is forgiving: `TryAdd` tries the road as drawn, and if there is no room for that (`NoRoom`, or a corner squeezed) tries again with whatever lands within 24 m, then 40 m, of the end of a piece going through that point (`Beside`), which is how a road too close beside a corner or a junction ends up through it; an overload returns the line the road will really follow and the places it will meet other roads, for the pen to draw. The end of a new road that would lie on another road's pavement lands on that road, and a new road passing a road end that close takes the end in (`Touching`); a stretch drawn over a road that is already there is left out and the rest built. Also a road dropped on another too close to a junction or road end for a junction of its own joins that one (`JoinPoint`), a road crossing right beside the end of a piece goes through the end, and when there is still no room the pen reaches for the nearest junction (`RoadPen.Eased`, on `NoRoom`). `TryReplace` changes a road to another type in place. A road's lanes each way are not fixed by the toolbar's list: `RoadType.With` makes a road of the same class with any count up to four each way (saved under a code such as `G1-3-2-m`: class, lanes each way, median), and `TryChangeLanes` gives the road under a point one lane more or fewer on that side, for its whole length; a road left with lanes one way only becomes one-way. A two-way road can have a turning lane down its middle in place of a median (`RoadType.CenterLane`, code ending `-t`, put in and taken out with `TrySetMiddleLane`; `a4t` on the toolbar is the five-lane avenue). It is built with half a lane of `inner` each side and `NetworkBuilder.PaveMiddle`, and at each end of the road where there is a left to take, the traffic arriving gets a left-turn lane in it at slot -1 for the last 70 m (never more than 45% of the road, so the two ends cannot overlap); the lanes beside it then no longer turn left. A road with a median gets the same thing a different way (`RoadType.Bays`, code ending `-b`, `a4b` on the toolbar): the median is a lane wide, and the builder stops the grass short of each left-turn lane, which is the only pavement in the gap. `TrySetMiddleLane` gives a road with a painted line the turning lane and a road with a median the left-turn lanes, and taking them away leaves the line or the median as it was. `TryReverseLane` turns the lane under a point round for the whole of its road: a one-lane one-way road runs the other way, a wider one-way road becomes two-way, and a two-way road passes a lane from one side to the other (the yellow line stays on the line the road was drawn along, so the pavement moves over where the sides come out unequal; a road cannot yet sit off its line). The fork rule also holds while a fork is still being drawn (`Streams`): two one-way roads may arrive together before the road that leaves is in. `Bend` gives the curve that leaves a point heading a given way and ends at another (`BendArrives` is the heading it arrives with, `FlowAt` the heading of a road at its open end), which is how curves are drawn so that each piece carries on from the last without a corner. `TryMove` moves a point and the ends of its roads with it (a curve keeps its shape) and `TryBend` bends a road through a point or straightens it; neither joins or crosses roads, which only drawing does. A piece can carry extra lanes (`EditSegment.Extras`, each an `ExtraLane`: a side and where it starts and ends along the piece): `TryAddLane` lays one between two points on the same road, outside the ordinary lanes on the side of the first point, `TryRemoveLane` takes one away, and `TryMoveLaneEnd` slides either end of one along its road (`AddedLanes` lists them, each as the line down its middle). Extra lanes on neighboring pieces that meet end to end are one lane (`ExtrasAlong`), and one that all but reaches the end of its road runs right to it and becomes a lane of the junction there. They are built as the builder's lanes that start late and end early, so traffic moves into them and back out as it does at a merge. Two cases tie one to a ramp (`WithExtras`): an extra lane that starts where a ramp merges in is the ramp's own lane carrying on, and one that runs up to an exit is a lane for the exit alone, with the lane inside it then going straight on only. That is how a lane gain, a lane drop, an exit-only lane and a fork are built. `TryAddRoundabout` lays a one-way, one-lane ring (counter-clockwise, its pieces flagged `EditSegment.Ring`), cuts the roads that cross it and removes what is inside; nodes on a ring are `Free`, so turning traffic gives way to traffic going round. Where a road carries on as a narrower one, the lanes the narrower road has no room for end about 70 m before they meet (`Narrowed`), so traffic moves over as at any lane drop instead of two lanes squeezing into one in the bend. Where a one-way road carries on as a wider one, the lanes nothing leads into start 14 m along (`Widened`), as where a lane is gained. Those are for roads that simply change kind; the lanes that line up are then the leftmost. A road built on from a particular lane is another thing (`SplitAt`, `Branch`, `SplitJoin`): the piece built on remembers the lane at that end (`EditSegment.LaneAtA` and `LaneAtB`, passed to `TryAdd` as `laneFrom` and `laneTo`, saved as `LA:` and `LB:`), and a point where every road but one carries such a number is a split: that one road is the trunk, each of the others a one-way branch that takes on some of the trunk's arriving lanes or feeds some of its leaving ones. A trunk may be two-way. The node is `Free`; each lane is told the road and the very lane it carries on as (`TrackSpec.Onto` and `Into`); and the branch does not meet the point in the middle but beside it, where its lanes are (`Branch.Off`, `NetworkBuilder.Attach`, with the branch's line moved over by that much at that end and evenly less along its length, `Displaced`). The player's complaint that led to this: a road pulled from the right lane went to the middle of the road and took the left lane. Lanes of the trunk nothing has been built on from end about 70 m early, as a lane drop, but keep their pavement and paint to the end of the road (`TrackSpec.Stub`); on a trunk too short for that (under about 100 m) they run to the end and close up there, giving way, with the lane beside them. A branch's lanes all start with the branch, fed or not, so every road is the width it was drawn from end to end. `LaneEnds` still lists the trunk's unbuilt lanes, so the other lanes can be built on from afterwards (the player found they lost their arrows). Branches may set off as close together as they like, since they start side by side. The trunk is the wider road: when a road with more lanes is built on from the end of a narrower one (or run in to its start), the two are turned about (`WiderIsTrunk`), the point moving over to the middle of the wider road so that nothing already built shifts. That leaves the wider road's other lanes open, which is how two single lanes are brought back together as two lanes side by side; the player tried it and the second road was squeezed into the lane of the first. The lanes a trunk's branches hold are dealt out again whenever one is added so that the leftmost road has the leftmost lane (`Uncross`), and no two cross. A one-way road run to such a point with no lane picked takes a lane that is still open (`TakeOpenLane`), and what a branch was built on from is forgotten when that road goes (`CleanTies`). `Faults` leaves a split alone. `LaneEnds` lists every lane still open to build on, at open road ends and at splits, with where it is and which way its traffic runs, and `LaneAt` finds the lane under a point partway along a road; the pen draws its arrows from these. The player can say what runs an intersection and post a speed limit. What runs it is kept per approach, on the end of the piece that arrives there (`EditSegment.ControlAtA`, `ControlAtB`: a light, a light with arrows, a stop sign, a yield sign, no sign), so it survives the road being cut or redrawn; `TrySetControl` sets it for the intersection that the lane under a point leads to, from a `Control` chosen as seen from that lane's road (`Stop` and `Yield` sign that road and the one straight across and leave the cross street without; `RightOfWay` is the other way about), `ControlAt` reads it back, and `ControlOf` turns it into a junction mode and a sign per arm. A speed limit (`EditSegment.Limit`, set with `TrySetSpeed` for a whole road, one of `SpeedLimits`) overrides the type's; pieces only carry on as one road if their limits match, and a piece drawn on from a posted road takes its limit. Two one-way roads that cross are an intersection, not a fork: `Streams` only counts roads that all arrive and leave as a weave if they run within 50 degrees of one another (a grid of one-way streets was once built with no lights at all). `ToWorld` builds everything else with automatic control: a bend where two different kinds of road meet, all-way stops where only 25 mph streets meet, a light otherwise; an open road end is a map edge, unless the map has an edge of its own (`Bound`, which levels and now the player's own map have): then only a road end on that edge, or one of a level's named ends, brings traffic in, and any other is a dead end where traffic turns round (the player asked for that). A one-way road that stops short is not one of the `Faults` (it was, and the red ring sat on every road the player had not finished): its traffic leaves the map there, and its lanes show their arrows. Roads are added to the builder in the order their first pieces were drawn; the traffic tests are sensitive to that order. It saves as one line of text.
  - Water: a level may have lakes and rivers (`EditableMap.Waters`, shapes made with `River` and `Lake`; `World.Waters`). The land is flat and so is the water, as the player wants no hills. A road on the ground cannot cross it (`InWater`); a bridge can once it is 3 m up, and a tunnel once it is 3 m down (`RampInWater` otherwise). `LevelOver` says a road drawn across water on the ground should be a bridge, and the pen builds it as one without being asked (`RoadPen.Over`): refusing would not be child-proof. Water comes with a level, like its edge, and is not saved with the drawing. `RoadView.AddWater` draws it as a flat blue sheet with a strip of sand round it (`Geo.Triangulate`), and nothing grows in it. There is no tool yet for the player to place water on their own map.
  - `Routes.cs`: for each way off the map, the seconds from every lane to it, and so the turn to take at each junction and whether one edge of the map can reach another. With `Simulation.UseRoutes` on, each arriving vehicle picks a destination it can reach (from `Simulation.Demand`, or any other exit) and follows the quickest route; `Arrived`, `TripSeconds` and `TripIdeal` record finished journeys. Off, vehicles turn at random, which is what the lab maps and most tests use.
  - `Levels.cs`: the puzzles (`Levels.All`), five so far; the fifth, The River, is the first with water. A `Level` lays its own roads (locked: `EditSegment.Locked`, free, not removable) coming in at named places (`EditableMap.Ends`, which become `Node.Name`) round a building area (`EditableMap.Bound`), lists the journeys traffic wants, and has a budget. `Connected` counts the journeys the roads make possible; `Stars` is 0 until every journey is possible, the cost is within budget and enough have been finished, then 1 to 3 by how little longer journeys took than on empty roads.
  - `LabWorld.cs`: the lab grid, described in its header comment. `Junction.cs`: one controller per node, with modes `Free` (a bend), `Signal`, `ProtectedLeft`, `AllWay` and `Signs` (a stop or yield sign on some arms, `Approach.Sign`, and none on the others; with none anywhere it is `NoSigns`). See "Rules of the road" below.
- `Assets/Scripts/Lab/`: the Unity side. `LabBootstrap` creates a `LabApp` when Play is pressed, in any scene, so there is no scene file. Meshes, materials, camera and light are built in code (`MeshKit`, `RoadView`, `CarViews`). `RoadView` lays the ground and the roads in textured surfaces (`SurfaceBuilder`): asphalt mapped from its place on the map, a gravel shoulder on roads of 55 mph and over, a curbed grass median, and crosswalk bars at intersections with a light or signs. A slower road gets only a lip of concrete along its edge (`CurbLip`, 7.5 cm high and 22 cm wide: the player wants no sidewalks and nothing that looks like a major curb), and beside every road the grass is a little lighter for 2.4 m, as if mown (`Verge`, drawn with `MeshKit.Brighten`, which lightens whatever is beneath it in proportion, so it holds in shadow and at night; `Outward` gathers the band to a point where it would fold over itself inside a tight corner). Do not put dark shading along the edges of roads: a mock-up had it and the player rejected it ("black shadows... they don't make sense"). Paint is drawn 1.4 times its true width (`PaintWidth`), or from the usual distance it is a pixel wide and looks like a plan. Roads of 14 m/s and over that are not highways (avenues, not side streets) get street lamps (`StreetLamps`: a post every 46 m behind the curb with an arm over the road, the two sides out of step, all of them in one mesh). A signal's heads hang upright from an arm over the road, one per lane, lamps toward the drivers they control and red at the top (the player asked for that: they had lain flat, facing the sky; from straight overhead only the housings show, so the lamp that is lit has a glow standing in front of it, `LaneHead.Halo`, and the colored bar at each stop line, which lights the road round it, tells the state). It hangs across the intersection from its stop line, over the mouth of the road that carries straight on, where there is one within about 25 degrees; where there is not (the stem of a T, the arms of a Y) it stands just past its own stop line, since across the junction there is only grass (`BuildSignal`). A stop sign or a yield sign stands upright on a post by the stop line of each road that has one, facing its traffic, and the bar across each lane at the stop line flashes red for a stop sign and yellow for a yield sign or an intersection with no signs. Sidewalks (a raised walk with a curb along slower roads and round their corners, `Node.Corners`) are switched off (`RoadView.Sidewalks`): the player wants the zones of a city to lay them later. The roads are meant to look newly built: even dark asphalt, bright paint and no wear (the player asked for the wheel tracks and grime to go). Under each tree goes soft dark shading (`ShadeBuilder`, drawn with `MeshKit.Shade`, which only ever darkens). Scenery (trees as a trunk under lumpy leaf-covered tops, and bushes, from `CanopyBuilder`, all in summer greens close enough to read as one color, rocks, and drifts of wild flowers, `FlowerMesh`, each a small flat shape just above the grass) is built in square patches whose meshes are kept between rebuilds; each square of ground decides what grows on it from its own position, so scenery stays put when roads change. Light: `LabApp.ApplyLight` sets a low warm sun (35 degrees up, for long shadows) and a cool sky light, and `FitShadows` draws shadows only as far as the camera can see, which keeps them sharp close in. Things that give off light are drawn twice: the lamp itself, unlit, and a glow round it that adds light to whatever is behind (`MeshKit.Glow`, shapes from `GlowBuilder`; a glow is three flat patches at right angles, so it reads as round from any side). There are no real point lights and no post-processing: the scripts cannot name the pipeline's types, and nothing here can check how bloom would look. A dark picture drawn first in `OnGUI` darkens the corners of the screen (`Vignette`). Day and night (the toolbar's switch, or K): `LabApp.dusk` runs from 0 to 1 over a second and a half, and `ApplyLight` passes it to `RoadView.Night` and `CarViews.Night`. At night the sun becomes a moon a third as strong, the street lamps light up with a pool of light under each, signs (unlit, so they would otherwise shine) dim, and every vehicle has headlights that light the road ahead and tail lights. `CarViews` gives each vehicle brake lights (on while slowing or stopped, and held on until the foot is nearly off the brake, so they do not flicker), turn signals and hazard lights. They are a small detail: each lamp is a thin plate against the end of the vehicle that changes color, with no glow at all by day and a faint one at the brake lights after dark (`GlowAt`). The first version had bright glows by day standing off the car, every signal flashing in step, and the player found it very annoying: keep lights dull and set into the vehicle, "like a color on the car". Each car's signals flash on its own beat (rate and phase from its number). In the simulation a signal comes on for a lane change, from wanting it until it is done, and for a turn only in the last 30 m, and some drivers never signal (`Car.Signals`, from the car's number and the driver's aggression). "Draw your own" in the map list opens the player's `EditableMap` in build mode and rebuilds the world after every edit. All the drawing itself is done by `RoadPen` (in `Ui/`, below): `LabApp` passes it the pointer's place on the ground, clicks and drags, rebuilds the world when it says the map changed, and copies its `Ghost` into a mesh each time that changes (`ShowGhost`); every frame it also moves the arrows that run along the road being drawn (`Ghost.Run`) and stands a marker, a post with a ball of light, at each of `Ghost.Posts` (`StandMarkers`, drawn over everything with `MeshKit.Overlay` so the road's own picture never hides them). `LabUi` draws the price of the road in hand on a white tag riding on the marker at the end being held (`RoadPen.Price`, `Detail`, `TagAt`), and the words in `Ghost.Labels` ("New intersection") over the places they belong. The left button only builds and picks things, and acts as it goes down (with the move tool, a press on a point or a road drags that). The right button drags the view, and a right-click that does not drag takes back the last click; the wheel zooms about the pointer; the middle button, or Q and E, turns the view (`yaw`, `tilt`; the player asked for the drag to be slowed to its present rate), W, A, S, D and the arrows move it, and N puts north back up. Keys that act while held are tracked from GUI key events (`held`). While building, a handful of cars drive around (`AmbientCars`) and are put back on their roads after each rebuild (`Carry`); Drive starts full traffic. The drawing is saved in `PlayerPrefs`, with undo and redo kept as saved copies. A puzzle level opens the same way (`LoadLevel`), with its place names, the edge of its building area, cost against budget and routes joined shown, and a card with its stars once solved. The UI is immediate-mode GUI (`LabUi.cs`) and reads mouse and keyboard from GUI events, so it works under either input setting. Anything in the UI that changes which controls a panel holds (loading a map, Build, Drive) is put in `later` and done in `Update`, because changing it between GUI passes makes Unity throw.
- `Assets/Scripts/Ui/`: the toolbar at the bottom of the screen and the hand that draws roads (namespace `Traffic.Ui`), with no Unity references. `RoadPen` holds the map being built, the tool, and the road half drawn, and turns clicks and drags into changes to the map. A straight road is a chain of clicks. A curve leaves the last point the way the road was already heading and sweeps round to the pointer; from a point with no single road to carry on from, one click first points it. Right-click goes back a step. The end of a road catches on roads and points within reach (in meters, or a few pixels if that is more), and a straight road nearly in line with the roads it starts from (straight on, square, halfway) is pulled into line when angle snapping is on. Roads are built off lanes, which the player called the number one thing missing from building. While the road tool is out, every lane that traffic leaves by at an open road end has a blue arrow (`Ghost.Knob`), and once a road is started every lane that sets out from one has a broken blue ring (`Ghost.Socket`); partway along a road the lane under the pointer shows one or the other when the road in hand is one-way. Nothing of this is on the ground once drawing is done. A road started from an arrow carries on from that lane: with as many lanes as the road has it is simply the road carrying on; narrower, the picked lane carries on (`fromLane`, then `SetLaneTie`); wider, it gains its new lanes on the side it is drawn toward, or on the left if drawn straight on (`RoadPen.LaneTie`). A one-way road started on a lane partway along and drawn the way that lane runs leaves along the lane (`laneStart`, `Sweep`), which the map then makes an exit on the right, or a fork on the left of a one-way road (there it sets off 0.3 radians aside, since a fork's roads must part); ended on a lane it is heading along, it sweeps in alongside and merges. Drawn square off a road it is an intersection as before. At a road end with more than one lane there is also a white ring just beyond the end (`Ghost.Whole`, `WholeEnds`): only a road started there takes the road as a whole and meets it in the middle. Anywhere else on a road end, with a one-way road in hand, the lane nearest the pointer is the one built on from, or brought in to (`LaneEndNear`, `LaneEndOver`): the player's rule is one lane at a time, and only what is picked on the toolbar. The road in hand is drawn where it will really lie, from the lane, and its tag says its lanes ("1 lane one-way"). Beside each open road end that traffic leaves by stands a plus on either side (`Ghost.Plus`, `PlusEnds`): clicked, the road carries on from there one lane wider on that side, whatever is chosen on the toolbar (`stroke`, `InHand`), until the player stops drawing; that is how a second lane is put beside a first. Pressing on a lane's arrow and pulling off to one side before letting go does the same (`Lift`, which `LabApp.EditLift` calls when the button comes up). Still to do: turning round at a two-way trunk whose arriving lanes have nothing built on from them yet (their traffic leaves the map there). The move tool drags points, bends roads, and slides either end of an added lane along its road (a point is taken hold of before a lane's end, and a lane's end before the road). With a grid on (`RoadPen.Grid`, lines 25, 50, 100, 250 or 500 ft apart, with every 250 ft drawn stronger; they were the land survey's 33, 165, 330 and 660 ft until the player asked for multiples of 25) every new point lands where two lines cross, a click on a road lands where the road meets the grid, the edge of the map stands in for a line that would fall just outside it, and the grid has the last word over angle snapping; `GridPicture` draws it into the ghost as faint lines, thinning them out from far away. The signs tool (`RoadPen.Signs`) picks a lane as the lane tool does and shows `SignCard`: what runs the intersection the lane leads to, and the speed limit on its road. The lane tool treats each lane as a thing of its own: it lights up the lane the pointer is on, for the whole length of its road (`EditableMap.LaneUnder`); a click picks that lane, and a card beside it (`LaneCard`) offers to add a lane on that side of the road or take one away, and, for a lane that leads to a light or stop signs, arrows to switch on and off the ways it may go, a button to put a turning lane down the middle of the road (on a road with a median, left-turn lanes in the median), and a button to run the lane the other way; the middle lane is picked like any other, and Remove takes it out; a drag along the side of a road adds a lane for just that part, or lengthens one that is there. The player wants every lane to be selectable and changeable by itself, and building to be simple enough for a child: prefer doing the sensible thing over refusing. On a map with an edge, a point at or beyond the edge lands on it (`ToEdge`), which is how a road is run off the map. Places `EditableMap.Faults` reports are ringed in red and explained under the pointer. The pen keeps a `Ghost`: flat colored triangles showing what the next click would do. The road about to be built is shown as itself (`Ghost.Road`): dark pavement at its real width along the line it will really follow, which is the map's answer and not always the line pointed out, with a line of teal light along each edge and a soft glow beyond, a yellow line down the middle of a two-way road, and open arrows running along it the way its traffic will go (one line each way on a two-way road; `Run` places them for a moment in time). It is red and still when it cannot be built. Where it will meet a road there is a pad of light (`Pad`), with the words "New intersection" unless there are more than three. The ghost also holds pins where the road starts and ends, with a standing marker asked for at each (`Stand`), and handles for the move tool. Everything is sized so outlines stay a few pixels wide however far out the view is. The pen also keeps a `Note` for beside the pointer (the reason something cannot be built) and, when the next click has a price, a `Price` and a `Detail` ("545 ft of street") for the tag. `LaneCard` is the lane tool's card. `Dock` lays out and draws the tools (road, lanes, signs, move, replace, bulldoze: large buttons with the name under the icon, `Labeled`, which the player asked for), undo, redo and clear, the day and night switch, a wide yellow Drive button (teal Build while driving), the tray of choices (shape, kind of road from `Dock.Roads`, which `LabApp.RoadTiles` fills from `RoadType.All`, height, angle snapping, grid) and the hover cards onto an `ISurface`; `LabUi.GuiSurface` is the Unity one, using the `GUI.DrawTexture` overload that rounds corners. `Icons` draws each icon in code with `Painter`.
- Textures: the ground, asphalt, sidewalk, gravel and foliage are made by `Tools/BakeArt/textures.py` from CC0 photo scans in `Art/ambientcg/`, each scan repeated small for close-up detail, moved to the color wanted on screen (`toned`), with only a faint slow drift of tone over it so the repeat does not show: fresh dark asphalt, clean concrete, one even green for the grass. `LabApp.ApplyLight` sets the sun and sky light so that flat ground in the open shows a texture at about its own brightness (measured from a player's screenshot, where the project turned out to be in gamma color space: lit color is texture color times the sun, as much of it as falls on flat ground, plus ambient). Keep that sum near 1 by day if the light is changed, or every color shifts. They are saved as JPEG data named `.bytes` in `Assets/Resources/Art/` and decoded by `ArtKit.Photo`, so Unity's texture import settings do not matter. Missing ones fall back to plain colors.
- Art: cars, trucks and rocks are CC0 models from Kenney's Car Kit and Nature Kit (the kit's trees are baked too but no longer drawn). The sources used are in `Art/` (outside `Assets/`, so Unity ignores them). `Tools/BakeArt/bake.py` (Python with numpy and Pillow) turns them into `Assets/Resources/Art/models.bytes`: vehicles scaled by one factor for the whole kit (`KIT_SCALE`, so they keep their proportions; they come out slightly shorter and wider than the simulation's 4.5 by 1.9 m car, and the truck's simulated size, 5.0 by 2.3 m, was set from the model) and facing +x, each face marked as paint (takes the vehicle's color), leaf (takes a leaf color) or a fixed palette color. `Assets/Scripts/Art/ArtFile.cs` reads the file (no Unity references) and `Lab/ArtKit.cs` builds meshes from it, all sharing one small point-filtered texture of flat colors. Nothing goes through Unity's model importer. If the file is missing, vehicles and trees fall back to plain shapes. The motorcycle (with its rider), the box truck, and the semi's tractor and its box and tank trailers have no model: `Lab/VehicleShapes.cs` builds them from boxes and drums (`SolidBuilder`), once each. (The motorcycle was two floating boxes, which the player called terrible.) Trucks carry the name and slogan of a made-up company on their sides (`Art/Livery.cs`, no Unity references: the names are jokes about traffic, the picture is painted in letters five dots by seven, `DotLetters`). One semi in three is a tanker, with a fuel company's name. The player asked for more kinds of truck with made-up slogans; more are welcome.
- `Assets/Tests/Sim/`: NUnit tests of the simulation.

## Behavior notes

- Right of way inside an intersection is a rank (`Simulation.Rank`). At a `Free` node (a way on to a roundabout, a fork, roads coming together) traffic going straight on outranks traffic turning, and where two ways straight on end in one lane the one that bends more gives way (`Lane.GiveWay`, set by `NetworkBuilder.MarkGiveWay`), and a turning car waits short of the traffic going by for as long as it takes: the `BoxWait` rule below does not apply there. A red-light runner waits for a gap, except a pushy one (`IsBully`), who outranks everyone once past the line; ordinary drivers who see that brake hard (`Scared`).
- A car only counts as inside an intersection once its nose is `Committed` (0.5 m) past the stop line. Cars outside hold at the line while someone is sitting in their path, and a car stuck inside stops giving way after a while (`BoxWait`), but only to cars that are themselves stopped or crawling: it never pulls out in front of traffic at speed, which it once did, into a broadside. These three rules exist to prevent standstills; the stress probes that found them ran 60 to 120 cars with special drivers for 10 simulated minutes.
- Roads follow US practice: 12 ft lanes (`LaneW` 3.66 m), 10 ft dashes with 30 ft gaps, and limits of 25, 35 and 65 mph (`CitySpeed`, `ArterialSpeed`, `HighwaySpeed`).
- A drawn road has a `Level`: 0 on the ground, 1 to 3 for a bridge, -1 to -3 for a tunnel, 5 m a level, reached over a ramp at each end (`EditableMap.RampFor`) and costing more (`CostTimes`). It crosses other roads without an intersection where there is 4.5 m between them, and can only be joined at its ends. `NetworkBuilder.Raise` lifts or sinks the road just added; strips and paint carry a height per point (`Heights`). `RoadView` draws a bridge as a deck with walls, and a tunnel as open road until it is 2.4 m down (`Covered`), then broken lines on the ground with a dark mouth at each end; `CarViews` hides cars that deep.
- A road added with `bridge: true` rises over whatever it crosses (`Lane.Peak`, `Car.Height`). Roads only interact at nodes, so traffic on and under a bridge ignores each other; crash detection skips pairs at different heights.
- `Node.Controlled` means a real intersection with a light or stop signs. Bends, ramp junctions and map edges are not controlled: no stop lines, no signals, not selectable.
- A driver looks ahead along their route as far as `Lookahead`, through as many pieces of road as that takes (`FindLeader`), and past a junction they have not chosen a turn at yet where the lane has only one way on (`RouteNext`). It was three lanes and one junction, which was enough when no road was shorter than 15 m; with pieces a few meters long drivers ran into the back of a queue they could not see.
- A car moving into the same lane as traffic that does not stop for it, at a junction with no light (a ramp with no lane of its own, two lanes closing into one), leaves a gap that fits the difference in speed: the difference over `JoinBraking` in seconds, on top of the usual margin, and it looks 7 seconds up the road to judge it. Without that, ramps on to a highway with no added lane were hit from behind.
- A driver changing lanes looks for the car ahead and the car behind in the next lane through the junction at either end and into the road beyond (`LeaderIn`, `FollowerIn`, `LookBack`), and ignores a car that is already moving out of that lane. Without this, drivers near a merge pulled out in front of fast traffic they could not see, or into a queue just past the junction.
- Accidents (`Simulation.Collide`): vehicles that overlap crash. Closing speed decides whether it is a fender bender (both stop, then drive on) or a wreck (blocks the road until towed, after `TowAfter`); the angle between them gives the `CrashKind`. Drivers go round a wreck when there is another lane (`Car.BlockedAhead`), and slow to look at one nearby (`Gawking`). With `CrashesEnabled` off, a car that would rear-end another is held behind it and counted in `Bumps`, and cars crossing paths pass through each other.

## Rules of the road

The player asked for rules that mimic Ohio's. These follow the Ohio Revised Code, read from codes.ohio.gov in November 2026; the section is cited where the rule is coded.

- Speed limits (4511.21(B)): `EditableMap.SpeedLimits` is the statute's list. 15 mph alleys, 20 school zones, 25 other streets in a municipal corporation, 35 state routes and through highways in town outside business districts, 50 controlled-access highways and expressways in town, 55 highways out of town, 60 rural divided highways, 65 freeways in urbanized areas and rural expressways without signals, 70 rural freeways. The toolbar's avenue is 40 mph, which is not on that list (a limit like it has to be set by an engineering study); it can be posted at 35.
- Stop and yield signs (4511.43): at a stop sign stop at the line, then give way to anything in the intersection or close enough to be an immediate hazard; at a yield sign slow to a reasonable speed and stop only if needed. `Simulation.MayEnter` makes the stop (or the slowing to `YieldSpeed`), and `JunctionStop` then holds a signed car at its line until its whole way across is clear (`atSign`), looking 7 seconds up the cross street. `Rank` puts unsigned arms above signed ones. Roads entering a through street get one or the other (4511.65).
- Left turns give way to oncoming traffic (4511.42): half a rank lower, with or without signs. On the through street of a signed intersection a left-turner waits in the middle as long as it takes (no `BoxWait` give-up), or it bumps into traffic that is only starting off.
- No signs at all (4511.41): of two who arrive from different streets at about the same time, the one on the left gives way. `NoSigns` runs on the all-way stop's first-come order, without the stop (cars come up at `YieldSpeed`), and with that rule for cars arriving within 1.5 s of each other; it lapses after 4 s so that four arriving together do not wait for ever. An earlier version ranked everyone equal and let them judge gaps at speed: it crashed often and then jammed.
- Signals (4511.13): right on red after a stop, and left on red from a one-way street into a one-way street (`OneWayToOneWay`), both yielding as at a stop sign (`Simulation.RightOnRed` switches both off). A left on green gives way to oncoming traffic; with arrows it goes on its own arrow.
- Do not enter an intersection without room to leave it (4511.712): `ExitHasSpace`.
- Slower traffic keeps right (4511.25(B)): `Simulation.KeepRight`. In `LaneChanges` a driver with no lane they have to be in leans a little to the right, and strongly when someone faster is close behind in the same lane (`holdingUp`). A driver held up by someone slower waits `PassingPatience` (3 s) for them to move over before going round on the right. Left-lane campers ignore all of it.
- A light that has gone out is treated as stop signs all round (4511.132): `Junction.Fail` and `Mend`, `Simulation.FailSignal`. The junction's mode becomes `AllWay` with `Failed` set, so every rule for an all-way stop applies; the heads stay up, dark, and the bars at the stop lines flash red. The player puts a light out from the intersection's panel while driving. Nothing puts one out by itself yet.
- No turn on red where a sign says so (4511.13(C)(4)): `Approach.NoTurnOnRed`, set per road end from the signs tool's card (`EditableMap.TrySetNoTurnOnRed`; kept as `NoRedBit` added to the end's control code, so it is saved, copied and split with it).
- School buses (4511.75): `VehicleKind.SchoolBus`. On a street of 41 mph or less, long enough and on the ground, a bus may have a stop (`Car.BusStop`, chosen as it turns on to the street): it pulls up at the curb, and for 8 to 13 seconds it is `Loading`. Everyone else stops at least ten feet short of it (`BusClearance`) and waits: traffic behind it in any lane, and traffic coming the other way unless the road has four lanes or more (`Lane.RoadLanes`). Someone already alongside, or too close to stop gently, carries on, and the drivers who run red lights run this too. `Simulation.ArrivalBuses` is their share of arrivals (none unless asked for; the lab asks for 2%, a puzzle for none). Two sections could not be read and are not relied on: 4511.39 (turn signals) and 4511.45 (emergency vehicles).

## Look

The player chose the look from a page of mock-ups: the "polished" one (the game as it is, lit and finished) with day and night, not a restyle. Roads stay clean and newly built. They turned down flat picture-book colors with outlines, and did not want the tabletop look (toy colors, blurred edges, the map as a slab). Nothing of this has been seen in Unity by its author: the player sends screenshots, and the strengths of the glows, the sun and the night are first guesses to be tuned from those.

## Conventions

- A car's `S` is the position of its center along its lane. Traffic drives on the right.
- Sim units are meters and seconds; the UI shows mph and feet. Sim (x east, y north) maps to Unity (x, height, z = y); models face +x.
- Keep behavior deterministic: all randomness goes through `Simulation.Rng`.
- `SignalLight` and `PathPose` are named to avoid clashing with `UnityEngine.Light` and `UnityEngine.Pose`.
- Keep the C# to language version 9. Avoid Unity APIs marked obsolete in 6000.6 (for example `FindObjectsByType` with a sort mode).

## Checks

There is no Unity editor in the cloud container, so nothing here proves how the game looks or that it compiles in the installed Unity. With the .NET 8 SDK (`apt-get install -y dotnet-sdk-8.0`):

- `dotnet test Tools/SimTests` runs the simulation tests, and two files beside the project file that test code outside the simulation's assembly: `RoadPenTests.cs`, which draws roads click by click through the pen, and `ArtFileTests.cs`, which checks the baked art file (vehicles fit the simulation's sizes, which way they face, triangles facing outward).
- `python3 Tools/BakeArt/bake.py preview.png` rebakes the art and draws every model to a picture. `python3 Tools/BakeArt/textures.py preview.png` rebakes the textures and draws a sample of each with a mock-up of a road made of them (a guide to the intended look, not a render of the game).
- `dotnet build Tools/LabCompile` type-checks all scripts against Unity 2021.3 reference assemblies. It catches typos and type errors only.
- `dotnet run --project Tools/UiPreview out.html` draws the toolbar in each state, every icon, and the road pen at work (its ghost over a small map, at each step of drawing, moving and replacing) to a web page (text in Arial, close to Unity's default GUI font). Screenshot it to check a change to the toolbar or to how drawing looks.

Unity generates `Packages/`, the rest of `ProjectSettings/` and the `.meta` files on first open. They are not committed yet.
