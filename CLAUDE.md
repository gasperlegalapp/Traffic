# CLAUDE.md: Traffic

A traffic game built in Unity 6 (project version 6000.6.3f1) on Windows 11. The scripts run under the Built-in Render Pipeline or URP: `MeshKit` picks each pipeline's own shaders, and the debug lines are a mesh, not `GL` calls, because URP does not call `OnRenderObject`. The current build is the Driver Lab: a few maps for testing individual driver behavior, and a road builder where the player draws their own. Puzzle levels, zones and events come later. The earlier web version (TypeScript and three.js) was removed once the Unity lab worked; it is in the git history.

## Layout

- `Assets/Scripts/Sim/`: the simulation (namespace `Traffic.Sim`). It has no Unity references, runs on a fixed 1/30 s step (`Simulation.Dt`) with a seeded `Rng`, and is replayable from a seed.
  - `Simulation.cs`: each step does signal updates, per-lane car lists, `Perceive` (each car picks an acceleration), lane changes, integration, crash detection.
  - `Driver.cs`: the six driver dials (`DriverParams`) plus three quirks (`Weave`, `Swerve`, `Distraction`), values derived from them, the IDM car-following formula, and the `SpecialDriver` presets (maniac, slowpoke, on phone, drunk).
  - `Vehicle.cs`: `VehicleSpec` for cars, trucks and motorcycles: size, and multipliers on acceleration, braking, speed, cornering and lane-change time. `Simulation.DeriveFor(car)` combines driver and vehicle. Vehicles longer than 6 m are posed along the line from tail to nose so they do not swing into the next lane in turns. (No vehicle is that long at the moment: the truck is a 5 m delivery truck, sized from its model.)
  - `Network.cs`: `Lane`, `Node`, `Approach` and `World`. Paths through an intersection are lanes of kind `Connector`; `Connectors.Link` finds where they conflict.
  - `NetworkBuilder.cs`: builds a network from nodes joined by roads. A road is straight unless given via points (`AddCurvedRoad` takes a traced centerline and trims it at each end), may be one-way, and has a speed limit. An intersection grows to fit roads that meet at a narrow angle, so they have separated by their stop lines; `Build` throws if that leaves under 15 m of road between two intersections. Each direction is a set of lanes in slots (0 next to the centerline); a lane can start late (turn pocket, added lane) or end early (merge). It generates connectors, turn assignments, pavement and paint. A node with one road is the edge of the map (`Node.Terminal`): arriving lanes are exits (`Lane.IsExit`) and leaving lanes are entries (`World.Entries`). A `Free` node with three or more roads is a ramp leaving or joining; ramps use `shiftA`/`shiftB` to attach at the highway's outside lane.
  - `Maps.cs`: the maps the lab can load (`Maps.All`): the street grid, the figure eight, and a town joined to a divided highway by a diamond interchange (highway through lanes are given `Turn.Straight` explicitly so they are not offered the ramps on the far side). `Simulation.Arrivals` brings vehicles in at entries at a rate that rises and falls; vehicles that run off an exit lane are removed and counted in `Left`.
  - `EditableMap.cs`: the map the player draws: points joined by roads at any angle, each straight or curved (a quadratic Bezier toward a control point), with a lane count (1 is a single lane running the way it was drawn, 2, 4) and a speed limit. `TryAdd` snaps ends, makes an intersection where a new road crosses an old one, splits a road that is drawn to or from (a curve splits into two curves), and refuses roads that are too short, too tight, doubled, or that meet another at under 40 degrees. `ToWorld` builds it with automatic control: a bend for two roads, all-way stops where only 25 mph streets meet, a light otherwise; an open road end is a map edge. It saves as one line of text.
  - `LabWorld.cs`: the lab grid, described in its header comment. `Junction.cs`: one controller per node, with modes `Free` (a bend), `Signal`, `ProtectedLeft` and `AllWay`.
- `Assets/Scripts/Lab/`: the Unity side. `LabBootstrap` creates a `LabApp` when Play is pressed, in any scene, so there is no scene file. Meshes, materials, camera and light are built in code (`MeshKit`, `RoadView`, `CarViews`). `RoadView` also lays a textured ground, a pale verge along every road, and scenery (woods, bushes, rocks) in square patches whose meshes are kept between rebuilds; each square of ground decides what grows on it from its own position, so scenery stays put when roads change. "Draw your own" in the map list opens the player's `EditableMap` in build mode and rebuilds the world after every edit. While building, a handful of cars drive around (`AmbientCars`) and are put back on their roads after each rebuild (`Carry`); Drive starts full traffic. The drawing is saved in `PlayerPrefs`, with undo and redo kept as saved copies. The UI is immediate-mode GUI (`LabUi.cs`) and reads mouse and keyboard from GUI events, so it works under either input setting. Anything in the UI that changes which controls a panel holds (loading a map, Build, Drive) is put in `later` and done in `Update`, because changing it between GUI passes makes Unity throw.
- `Assets/Scripts/Ui/`: the toolbar at the bottom of the screen (namespace `Traffic.Ui`), with no Unity references. `Dock` lays out and draws the tools, the tray of road choices and the hover cards onto an `ISurface`; `LabUi.GuiSurface` is the Unity one, using the `GUI.DrawTexture` overload that rounds corners. `Icons` draws each icon in code with `Painter`.
- Art: cars, trucks, trees, bushes and rocks are CC0 models from Kenney's Car Kit and Nature Kit. The sources used are in `Art/` (outside `Assets/`, so Unity ignores them). `Tools/BakeArt/bake.py` (Python with numpy and Pillow) turns them into `Assets/Resources/Art/models.bytes`: vehicles scaled by one factor for the whole kit (`KIT_SCALE`, so they keep their proportions; they come out slightly shorter and wider than the simulation's 4.5 by 1.9 m car, and the truck's simulated size, 5.0 by 2.3 m, was set from the model) and facing +x, each face marked as paint (takes the vehicle's color), leaf (takes a leaf color) or a fixed palette color. `Assets/Scripts/Art/ArtFile.cs` reads the file (no Unity references) and `Lab/ArtKit.cs` builds meshes from it, all sharing one small point-filtered texture of flat colors. Nothing goes through Unity's model importer. If the file is missing, vehicles and trees fall back to plain shapes. Motorcycles are still plain shapes.
- `Assets/Tests/Sim/`: NUnit tests of the simulation.

## Behavior notes

- Right of way inside an intersection is a rank (`Simulation.Rank`). A red-light runner waits for a gap, except a pushy one (`IsBully`), who outranks everyone once past the line; ordinary drivers who see that brake hard (`Scared`).
- A car only counts as inside an intersection once its nose is `Committed` (0.5 m) past the stop line. Cars outside hold at the line while someone is sitting in their path, and a car stuck inside stops giving way after a while (`BoxWait`). These three rules exist to prevent standstills; the stress probes that found them ran 60 to 120 cars with special drivers for 10 simulated minutes.
- Roads follow US practice: 12 ft lanes (`LaneW` 3.66 m), 10 ft dashes with 30 ft gaps, and limits of 25, 35 and 65 mph (`CitySpeed`, `ArterialSpeed`, `HighwaySpeed`).
- A road added with `bridge: true` rises over whatever it crosses (`Lane.Peak`, `Car.Height`). Roads only interact at nodes, so traffic on and under a bridge ignores each other; crash detection skips pairs at different heights.
- `Node.Controlled` means a real intersection with a light or stop signs. Bends, ramp junctions and map edges are not controlled: no stop lines, no signals, not selectable.
- Accidents (`Simulation.Collide`): vehicles that overlap crash. Closing speed decides whether it is a fender bender (both stop, then drive on) or a wreck (blocks the road until towed, after `TowAfter`); the angle between them gives the `CrashKind`. Drivers go round a wreck when there is another lane (`Car.BlockedAhead`), and slow to look at one nearby (`Gawking`). With `CrashesEnabled` off, a car that would rear-end another is held behind it and counted in `Bumps`, and cars crossing paths pass through each other.

## Conventions

- A car's `S` is the position of its center along its lane. Traffic drives on the right.
- Sim units are meters and seconds; the UI shows mph and feet. Sim (x east, y north) maps to Unity (x, height, z = y); models face +x.
- Keep behavior deterministic: all randomness goes through `Simulation.Rng`.
- `SignalLight` and `PathPose` are named to avoid clashing with `UnityEngine.Light` and `UnityEngine.Pose`.
- Keep the C# to language version 9. Avoid Unity APIs marked obsolete in 6000.6 (for example `FindObjectsByType` with a sort mode).

## Checks

There is no Unity editor in the cloud container, so nothing here proves how the game looks or that it compiles in the installed Unity. With the .NET 8 SDK (`apt-get install -y dotnet-sdk-8.0`):

- `dotnet test Tools/SimTests` runs the simulation tests, and `ArtFileTests.cs` beside the project file, which checks the baked art file (vehicles fit the simulation's sizes, which way they face, triangles facing outward).
- `python3 Tools/BakeArt/bake.py preview.png` rebakes the art and draws every model to a picture.
- `dotnet build Tools/LabCompile` type-checks all scripts against Unity 2021.3 reference assemblies. It catches typos and type errors only.
- `dotnet run --project Tools/UiPreview out.html` draws the toolbar in each state, and every icon, to a web page (text in Arial, close to Unity's default GUI font). Screenshot it to check a toolbar change.

Unity generates `Packages/`, the rest of `ProjectSettings/` and the `.meta` files on first open. They are not committed yet.
